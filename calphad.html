mini-CALPHAD

Phase equilibria of binary alloys, read from the same TDB thermodynamic databases that pycalphad uses. The Lean checker certifies the true global equilibrium of the database model — the lowest Gibbs energy over every possible combination of phases — not only the solution the driver happened to find. Since 4 October the checker reads the TDB file itself, so its conclusions are about the model the database defines, and a second kind of certificate proves that a phase has no miscibility gap. Goal G3, set on 1 October 2026, is feature parity with pycalphad 0.11.1.

01

The model

SystemBinary A–B at fixed temperature and pressure; composition x = xB ∈ [0, 1]. Each phase has a Gibbs energy G(x) per mole of atoms, as pycalphad’s GM.
SolutionsOne mixing sublattice, the others vacancies only: ideal mixing RT(x log x + (1 − x) log(1 − x)) plus a Redlich–Kister excess x(1 − x) Σ Lk(1 − 2x)k.
MagnetismOptional Inden–Hillert–Jarl term RT log(1 + β(x)) g(Tc(x)/T), with Tc and β Redlich–Kister sums of the TC and BMAGN parameters, divided by the antiferromagnetic factor where negative. The two branches of g meet at T = Tc with equal value and slope — an identity Lean proves for every structure factor p.
CompoundsStoichiometric: they exist at one exact composition, such as 2/3.
Sublattice phasesCompound-energy-formalism phases with one or two mixing sublattices (A,B), the other sublattices filled by A, B or vacancies (CAL-C06, C07), such as θ = (Al)2(Al,Cu)1 or the Laves phase C15 = (Cu,Mg)2(Cu,Mg)1. With one mixing sublattice the composition is an affine function of its site fraction; with two, the Gibbs energy of the phase is the minimum over the internal states (two site fractions) that satisfy the composition constraint, as in pycalphad, and the checker runs a two-dimensional branch and bound on the square of internal states.
ParametersPiecewise SGTE expressions in T. The range valid at T is picked (Tlo ≤ T < Thi, as in pycalphad) and function references are expanded; each term is c·Tn or c·Tn log T, with c the exact product of the TDB’s decimal strings.
Not yetThe ordered phase of an order–disorder pair (the disordered phase is computed as in pycalphad), interstitial phases, three or more mixing sublattices, sublattice phases with missing endmembers or reciprocal parameters, magnetic sublattice phases, molecular species made only of A and B, more than two components. Such phases are skipped and listed in the report; the conclusions hold for the phases taken into account.

Since 4 October the checker reads the TDB file itself. With --tdb — the mode the driver uses whenever it verifies a run from a TDB file — Lean’s modelOfTdb builds the model from the file (temperature ranges, function references, species, phase types, site ratios, magnetic constants) and the checker requires it to equal the certificate’s model (CAL-I01, B02), so the driver’s translation is no longer trusted. What remains trusted (assumption A-CAL-01) is that this Lean definition, about 800 lines, reads a TDB file as intended; the benchmark compares it with the driver on every binary system of COST 507 and checks every phase’s G(x) against pycalphad to a relative 5×10⁻¹² (pycalphad adds 10⁻⁹ to Tc to avoid a singularity; the certificate uses the exact continuous form). Without --tdb a certificate certifies the exact model written in it.

02

What is certified

The equilibrium Gibbs energy G*(x₀) is the infimum of the total Gibbs energy over all feasible configurations: any finite collection of phases, compositions and amounts with the right overall composition. Geometrically it is the lower convex hull of all phase curves at x₀.

An equilibrium is a feasible configuration together with a common tangent ℓ(y) = μA(1 − y) + μBy that lies below every phase and touches the configuration; μA and μB are the chemical potentials. Lean proves that equilibria minimise the total Gibbs energy (CAL-C02) and that one always exists (CAL-C03).

A Gibbs-energy minimiser can stop in a local minimum, miss a phase or miss a miscibility gap. The checker’s statements are about every feasible configuration and every equilibrium, so such a mistake can only make the check fail or widen the intervals — never produce a wrong statement.

Worked example: a miscibility gap in Al–Zn

Al–Zn at 600 K and x(Zn) = 0.3, with the Mey (1993) database shipped with pycalphad. At this temperature the fcc solution splits in two: the equilibrium is two fcc phases with about 22% and 49% zinc. The checker proves that LIQUID and HCP_A3 appear in no equilibrium, confines the fcc compositions of every equilibrium to two windows at most 3×10⁻⁷ wide, pins G* and both chemical potentials to the last printed digit, 10⁻⁹ J/mol, and gives each phase’s driving force to the same precision. The run, check included, takes about one second.

04

Magnetism: the Cr–Fe miscibility gap

In Cr–Fe the bcc solution is ferromagnetic on the iron side (Tc = 1043 K, β = 2.22) and antiferromagnetic on the chromium side, where TC and BMAGN are negative and are divided by the antiferromagnetic factor. The magnetic energy is what splits the bcc phase into a Cr-rich and an Fe-rich solution below about 860 K. At 600 K and x(Fe) = 0.5 the checker certifies the two compositions to windows 10⁻⁷ wide in about two seconds.

The magnetic term is not a polynomial and has no convenient second derivative, so the checker bounds it on each interval with secant slopes instead of derivatives: log(1 + β), the antiferromagnetic convention, the powers tn and t−n, and both branches of the Inden–Hillert–Jarl function each have rational bounds on the slope between any two points of an interval, proved in Lean (CAL-C04). The resulting lower bound is exact up to a term of the order of the interval width squared, which is enough for the branch and bound to converge.

05

Phase diagrams

--map T1:T2:dT computes an isothermal section at each temperature of a grid: the driver walks the lower convex hull and splits [0, 1] into single-phase regions and common tangents, refines each tangent by Newton’s method, and writes one point-equilibrium certificate at the midpoint of every region. For a two-phase region the two windows of its certificate are the certified phase boundaries at that temperature, and every other phase is proved absent there.

Benchmark: Pb–Sn 300–600 K (43 regions), Al–Zn 400–900 K (35, including the fcc miscibility gap), Cu–Mg 600–1300 K (31), Cr–Fe 400–900 K (31) and Al–Fe 700–1700 K (63 regions, 34 common tangents, with the γ loop near pure iron). All 203 certificates pass, every boundary window is at most 2×10⁻⁶ wide, and pycalphad’s equilibrium gives the same phases and compositions at every certificate point. The certified boundary points lie on pycalphad’s binplot curves; in Al–Fe between 1300 and 1430 K binplot omits the Al2Fe region, which pycalphad’s own equilibrium and our certificates both show.

Since 5 October each grid temperature also gets a partition certificate (CAL-C09, B05). The slope of the equilibrium tangent is monotone in the overall composition, so point-equilibrium anchors bracket the tangent at every x₀ in between, and the checker proves, for every x₀ in [0, 1], which phases any equilibrium can contain: a table of single-phase regions, two-phase regions with their boundary windows, and narrow unresolved strips at the phase boundaries, covering [0, 1]. Benchmark: 7 systems at 67 temperatures, 174 single-phase and 201 two-phase regions, the widest unresolved strip 5.4×10⁻⁸; pycalphad’s equilibrium at 3346 points of a composition grid falls in a certified region with the same phases every time.

The partition is certified at each grid temperature; what happens between grid temperatures is not. A single-phase region proves “only this phase, at compositions in the window”; a miscibility gap inside that window is excluded only by a convexity certificate.

06

No miscibility gap: convexity certificates

A point-equilibrium certificate can prove that a phase has a miscibility gap at (T, x₀) — its windows exclude x₀ — but not that it has none at a lower temperature, so the temperature at which a gap appears had a proved bound on one side only. Since 4 October a second kind of certificate supplies the other side: for a non-magnetic substitutional solution it proves a·G″(x) > 0 on (0, 1), so G is strictly convex on [0, 1] (CAL-C05).

At a fixed T the parameters split exactly into P₀(x) + (log T)·P₁(x) with exact rational polynomials, so a·G″ = P₀″ + (log T) P₁″ + aRT(1/x + 1/(1 − x)); log T enters only as a coefficient between rational bounds. The checker runs its own branch and bound over [0, 1] — the driver supplies only the model, again bound to the TDB file — and prints CONVEX phase T (CAL-B03). Lean then proves that in any equilibrium, at any composition, all parts of that phase have the same composition, and that if it is the only phase, the single-phase state is the equilibrium at every x₀. A certificate takes 0.1–0.2 s to check.

Together with the point-equilibrium certificates this brackets the onset of a gap from both sides. In the benchmark — the liquid gaps of COST 507 Al–Ce, Fe–Si, Mg–Si and Cu–Si and of the Al–Ni database of Dupin (2001), and the fcc gap of Al–Zn — the highest temperature at which the checker proves convexity is located to 1 K (for Al–Zn, whose gap lies below, the lowest), and pycalphad’s G″ changes sign within the next kelvin every time.

CONVEX is a statement about one temperature; nothing is certified between two temperatures, so whether the phase changes only once inside a bracket is not proved. CAL_FAIL is not a proof that a gap exists — that comes from the windows of a point equilibrium. Magnetic phases are not covered yet: they would need bounds on the second derivative of the magnetic term, while CAL-C04 bounds only secant slopes.

07

How the checker works

Everything is exact rational arithmetic. The driver contributes only a candidate tangent, a candidate configuration, probe points and a point near each phase’s minimum.

  1. 1

    Parameter intervals and the tangent

    log T is enclosed by rational series bounds shared with mini-DFT; each solution phase becomes a rational polynomial plus the ideal mixing term (and the magnetic term), with a uniform rounding error. In single-phase regions the checker replaces the driver’s double-precision tangent by its own rational tangent at x₀ — soundness holds for any tangent, so this needs no new proof, only sharpens the result.

  2. 2

    Branch and bound below every phase

    On each interval, a lower bound for G − ℓ comes from exact Taylor coefficients of the polynomial, the proved quadratic bound on the mixing term (CAL-C01), the secant-slope bound on the magnetic term (CAL-C04) and the exact minimum of a quadratic. Each phase gets its own lower bound ei; their minimum e ≤ 0 bounds all phases from below.

  3. 3

    Interval for G*

    Every feasible configuration has total Gibbs energy ≥ ℓ(x₀) + e (CAL-C02); the certificate’s configuration, with amounts from the lever rule, gives the upper end.

  4. 4

    Chemical potentials

    Any equilibrium tangent passes through the G* interval at x₀ and stays below the phases at probe points on both sides; that pins its slope, hence μA and μB — to about 10⁻⁹ J/mol in two-phase regions and 10⁻⁸ to 10⁻⁷ J/mol in single-phase regions.

  5. 5

    Driving forces

    Any equilibrium tangent is within κ of the certificate’s on [0, 1], so min (Gi − ℓ*) ≥ ei − κ, and the driver’s point near the minimum gives the other end.

  6. 6

    Composition windows

    Phases present satisfy G − ℓ ≤ κ. A second branch-and-bound pass excludes every interval whose lower bound exceeds κ; what remains are the windows.

The bisection rule affects only speed and sharpness. Soundness depends only on two facts the proof checks: every excluded interval has a lower bound above the threshold, and the intervals cover [0, 1].

Research result: a certified audit of CALPHAD databases

Two questions floating-point software cannot settle: over what range of temperature and composition does a published database give physically sensible equilibria, and where does a general-purpose solver miss the true global equilibrium? Both need statements such as “no lower assemblage exists” and “this phase does not appear”, which is exactly what the certificates provide. The audit takes every binary system of the 17 databases shipped with pycalphad, COST 507 among them, on a grid from 300 K in steps of 100 K up to the database’s upper limit (at most 6000 K) and x = 0.02, 0.06, …, 0.98; it writes a certificate at every point, checks it in --tdb mode against the TDB file itself, and runs pycalphad on the same phases.

316 binary systems and 246 650 grid points, every one with a certificate the Lean checker accepted for the model its TDB file defines.

Anomalous liquid miscibility gaps

In five systems the liquid loses convexity at high temperature, so a liquid miscibility gap opens as the temperature rises — an artefact of extrapolated parameters, which these files declare valid up to 6000 K. Each onset is bracketed to 1 K with a proof at both ends: a convexity certificate below, and above, a point equilibrium of the liquid alone whose windows exclude x₀.

In COST 507 Fe–Mg (1000 K, x = 0.5) the phases agree but pycalphad’s Gibbs energy is at least 1.47 J/mol too high. At the other points, about 250 000, pycalphad’s value lies inside the Lean interval or less than 1 J/mol above it.
  • pycalphad misses a Laves phaseWith phases of two mixing sublattices included (CAL-C07), in COST 507 Cr–Ti at 300 K and x(Ti) = 0.34 pycalphad returns a single hcp phase (Gibbs energy +812.7 J/mol); the certificate proves that the equilibrium is the C15 Laves phase plus ALM_D019 at −9006.96 J/mol, so pycalphad is at least 9.8 kJ/mol too high (32 grid points in all). At 4 points in Mn–Ti pycalphad stops at a local internal state of the C14 Laves phase (at least 4.5 J/mol too high).
  • A driver bug found by the auditMolecular species made only of A and B — such as CU2 in COST 507’s CU6Y — were dropped as if they were unrelated elements, so the driver certified a different model from the TDB file; pycalphad’s Gibbs energy fell up to 128 J/mol below the Lean lower bound, which is how the model mismatch showed. It is fixed, and the Lean TDB reader marks such phases unsupported.

Limits: the conclusions hold at the listed grid points only; boundaries and gap edges between grid points are not certified. Phases the driver cannot model yet (three or more mixing sublattices, sublattice phases with missing endmembers, order–disorder, interstitial) are skipped — conclusions that something is not an equilibrium are unaffected, but “this phase appears” holds only among the phases included. Whether an anomaly is a defect needs a person: a real liquid gap (as in Al–Pb) or a compound stable above both melting points can be physical. The reports and representative certificates are archived in research/calphad-audit/ and re-checked by make verify-all.

Open gaps

  • From TDB file to certified modelSince 4 October the checker builds the model from the TDB file in Lean (CAL-I01, B02), so the driver’s translation is out of the trust chain. What remains is whether Lean’s definition reads a TDB file as intended (A-CAL-01): it is cross-checked against the driver and pycalphad (T0), with no stronger evidence. A certificate checked without --tdb — for instance when the TDB is piped in — still certifies only the model written in it. Since 7 October the --tdb mode also prints the TDB file’s SHA-256 (CAL-I04), and all names are escaped, so forged phase names can no longer inject output lines (CAL-I05). Earlier, fuzzing had found inputs the driver translated into a different model that the checker then accepted (3*T**1.5 read as 3T + 0.5, truncated coefficients, a dropped phase), and the database audit found dropped molecular species; these were fixed, and the Lean reader follows the same rules for what to reject or skip.
  • No miscibility gap: magnetic phases and between temperaturesConvexity certificates cover non-magnetic substitutional solutions at a single temperature. Magnetic phases need second-derivative bounds on the magnetic term; nothing is certified between two temperatures; CAL_FAIL is not a proof of a gap.
  • Phase diagrams between grid temperaturesSince 5 October the partition certificate covers every composition at each grid temperature; temperatures between grid points are not certified, and a miscibility gap inside a single-phase window needs a separate convexity certificate.
  • Order–disorder, interstitial, further sublattice and multicomponent modelsNot yet defined in Lean (phases with one or two mixing sublattices are supported). The driver skips such phases and lists them; the conclusions hold only for the phases included. Magnetic phases whose TC or BMAGN depends on log T are also skipped.
  • Thermodynamic properties at equilibriumSince 6 October G, S, H and Cp are certified for a phase at fixed composition, as pycalphad’s calculate. Not yet: the equilibrium values (pycalphad’s equilibrium with HM, SM, CPM, the phases weighted by their amounts); the derivatives of a two-mixing-sublattice phase under internal equilibrium (the certificate gives the fixed-internal-state values); magnetic phases at T = Tc, where Cp jumps; and, in --tdb mode, a temperature on a breakpoint of the piecewise expressions.
  • The driver itselfIts grid search and Newton iterations are untrusted (T0) and marked so in the report; every result passes through the checker.
