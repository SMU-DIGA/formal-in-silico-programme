How each result is made

Every case is a real run. The untrusted C driver computes a candidate solution and writes a certificate; the proved Lean checker re-checks the certificate in exact rational arithmetic and prints rigorous intervals and error bounds; then an established code in the field solves the same problem independently, and we check that its answer falls inside the Lean intervals.

Every number in a “Certified by Lean” table is printed by the checker, whose soundness theorem guarantees it for the problem the certificate describes (lower bounds rounded down, upper bounds up). The comparison with the reference code is supporting evidence only and is outside the chain of trust. Every archived certificate is re-checked by the current checkers in make verify-all, with one labelled exception: the archived production record of the S1 silicon band gap (one check takes about 8 hours on 32 cores and the certificate is 253 MB, not in the repository), for which only the sha256 of the archived output is verified and the ledger ids are given.

Real silicon: the LDA SCF solution on a 4×4×4 k-point mesh, with bands at every k point DFT

Three-dimensional plane-wave Kohn–Sham LDA: diamond-structure silicon (a = 10.26 bohr) with a real GTH-LDA pseudopotential (CP2K’s Si q4, including the nonlocal part), Slater exchange plus PW92 correlation, zero temperature, a standard-shifted Monkhorst–Pack 4×4×4 mesh (64 k points) and the cutoff |k + G|² ≤ 17/2 in units of (2π/a)² (23–29 plane waves per k point), a 12³ density grid. The untrusted Python driver solves and writes a certificate; the Lean checker reduces the 64 k points to 10 representative blocks with the point group, proves that the discrete Kohn–Sham equations on the full mesh have exactly one zero-temperature SCF solution near the certificate centre, and bounds its chemical potential, the 16 bands at every k point and the band gap on the mesh (DFT-M75–M77, K21–K28, T4; the DFTK alignment DFT-M78 is T0). The cutoff is low, so the numbers are not converged silicon; the point is the whole chain — a real pseudopotential, k points, symmetry and exchange–correlation all inside a proved checker.
