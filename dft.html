The T4 claims are the soundness theorems of the certificate checkers (which run in exact rational arithmetic, so no floating point is involved) and the Frama-C/WP proofs about the trusted C kernel: no run-time errors (DFT-F03) and, since 6 October, the floating-point correctness of its interval arithmetic on the C code under an IEEE 754 model — the charge interval, the residual bound and the inertia count (DFT-F01, F02; the step from the exact elimination to the eigenvalue count is the Lean theorem exactElim_inertia). Three claims rest on paper proofs (T1, in domains/dft/notes/; no published result covers the 1D periodic Hartree kernel directly): continuum well-posedness of 1D rHF, the a priori plane-wave estimate and the local convergence of linear mixing for α < 2/λmax of the dielectric operator, all at their target. The equality of the truncation limit with the continuum energy (DFT-C02) is machine-proved since 8 October (T3): the continuum model is written in Fourier space, and its equivalence with the operator model on H¹ is assumption A-DFT-08. The Lean–Rocq interface claim DFT-I01 was withdrawn on 6 October, when decision D1 chose Frama-C rather than Rocq.

M2 · 1D physics terms

FeatureStatusWhat is certified
LDA exchange-correlation (Slater exchange, PW92 correlation)DoneEvaluated on the same real-space grid as DFTK (M = 4K + 1 points); six systems — zero temperature, Fermi–Dirac, Marzari–Vanderbilt, Gaussian with Monkhorst–Pack k-points, bands and DOS — agree with DFTK in energy, every component and every eigenvalue. The energy is no longer convex, so there is no variational certificate; for Slater exchange, with or without PW92 correlation, at the Γ point with any finite-temperature smearing (Fermi–Dirac, Gaussian, MV, or MP of any order), the checker proves that the nonlinear SCF equations have exactly one solution within about 10⁻¹² of the computed one and encloses its chemical potential, energy (exchange-correlation included) and free energy (DFT-X01–X15). For PW92 the correlation is written in s = √rs, where every derivative of its logarithm becomes a rational function regular at s = 0; the checker encloses it by interval arithmetic and bounds |v′| and |v″| for all densities above the grid minimum. The Fermi–Dirac occupation 1/(1 + ex) has its own proved derivative bounds, and its entropy is proved equal to the binary entropy used by the variational certificates (DFT-X16, X17). DFTK’s Fermi level, energy and free energy fall inside the Lean intervals. With Monkhorst–Pack k-points the certificate describes the equivalent supercell on a grid of nk·M points: shifting by one cell is then a grid shift, so the exchange-correlation potential is equivariant, the unique solution is cell-periodic, and the per-cell energy and free energy — exchange-correlation integrated on the cell grid, as DFTK does — are enclosed (DFT-X18–X20); the supercell grid must satisfy Ms ≥ 4Ks + 1, so these runs use M = 4K + 3, and DFTK on the same grid lands inside the intervals. Since 6 October also at zero temperature (certificate fis-lda0): the aufbau occupation is a step, so the checker replaces it by a smooth step that equals it outside (−1, 1) and checks that the Hartree–exchange-correlation Hamiltonian has no eigenvalue within the gap half-width T of μ₀ anywhere in the ball; there the smooth problem and the zero-temperature problem coincide, so the unique solution in the ball is the spectral projector below the gap, with N electrons, and its energy is enclosed (DFT-X21–X25), at the Γ point and with Monkhorst–Pack k-points.
Collinear spin (LSDA), magnetic termDoneSince 6 October, a first version: --spin collinear --xc lda_x --nelec N --bfield B, at the Γ point and finite temperature, with spin-polarised Slater exchange, one chemical potential for both spins, a free magnetisation and an optional uniform Zeeman field B. Each spin has its own density matrix γs = f((H[ρ↑ + ρ↓] + Vxc,s − μ ∓ B)/T), with tr γ↑ + tr γ↓ = N. The two spins form a block-diagonal problem, and the Newton–Kantorovich argument of the LDA certificate carries over block by block: the checker proves that the LSDA SCF equations have exactly one solution within 3×10⁻¹⁴–1×10⁻¹² of the computed one and encloses its magnetisation ν = tr γ↑ − tr γ↓ and chemical potential (DFT-X26–X28). DFTK’s magnetisation and Fermi level (collinear, Xc([:lda_x]), the Zeeman field as a spin-dependent external potential) fall inside the Lean intervals on four systems. DFTK’s Magnetic term is a vector potential, not a Zeeman field, and is not done. Since 7 October: Monkhorst–Pack k-points (--nk, an equivalent-supercell certificate; per-cell magnetisation intervals of width 2×10⁻¹³–5×10⁻¹³, DFT-X35–X37), zero temperature at the Γ point (fis-lsda0: exact integer magnetisation and a certified common gap of both spins, DFT-X40–X42; DFTK’s Smearing.None gives the same ν), and machine-proved bounds on the LSDA energy and free energy at the SCF solution (DFT-X45, X46; the driver’s floating-point E, F agree with DFTK to 7×10⁻¹⁵). 03

What a certified run reports today

Rigorous intervals for

  • the discrete ground-state energy, and the truncation limit with a bound on the discretisation error; since 8 October the truncation limit is proved equal to the ground energy of the continuum model, so the LIMIT_OK interval also contains the continuum energy (DFT-C02, C09)
  • the energy under the true Gaussian potential, Fourier tail included
  • the ground-state density (Coulomb norm and pointwise) and the Hartree potential
  • every eigenvalue of the ground-state Hamiltonian, and the gap
  • kinetic, external and Hartree energy components
  • band energies along a path and density-of-states values, at zero temperature and for finite-temperature SCF solutions (rHF and LDA), at the Γ point and, since 6 October, for k-point runs, where the primitive-cell Bloch Hamiltonian is built from the block average of the supercell solution
  • free energy, entropy and energy at finite temperature
  • for MP (any order) and MV smearing: a locally unique SCF solution, its chemical potential, energy and free energy
  • for LDA (Slater exchange, or Slater exchange with PW92 correlation) at finite temperature, with any of the four smearings, at the Γ point or with k-points: a locally unique solution of the nonlinear SCF equations, its chemical potential, energy including the exchange-correlation energy, and free energy
  • for LDA at zero temperature, when the gap stays open over the ball: a locally unique solution that is the spectral projector below the gap, the gap itself and the energy (since 6 October)
  • for collinear-spin LSDA (spin-polarised Slater exchange, or with spin-interpolated PW92 correlation, which since 8 October has all the same results; optional uniform Zeeman field) at the Γ point and finite temperature: a locally unique LSDA SCF solution, its magnetisation and its chemical potential (LSDA_MAG, LSDA_MU; since 6 October), with k-points and at zero temperature with an exact integer magnetisation and a common gap (LSDA0_*), and since 7 October its energy and free energy (LSDA_E, LSDA_F), per-spin bands and DOS (LSDA_BAND, LSDA_DOS, LSDA_DOS_TOT) and, for a Gaussian potential at Γ, the same for the named potential (GAUSS_LSDA*)
  • forces on Gaussian sites (--pot sites --forces, rHF at the Γ point, zero temperature or Fermi–Dirac and Gaussian smearing): an interval for each force FORCE_OK and for their sum FORCE_SUM (since 8 October)
  • for closed-shell Hartree–Fock (--xc hf, finite temperature, Γ point): a locally unique HF SCF solution and its chemical potential (HF_OK, since 8 October)
  • per-cell values for k-point runs, through the equivalent supercell

Every certificate written since 4 October also names its external potential, and the checker prints the problem it certified before any result (next section). Since 7 October “locally unique” means the SCF solution itself: every SCF solution is a zero of the auxiliary map the certificate checks, so the ball contains exactly one SCF solution (DFT-T32–T37).

04

Problem binding: which potential was certified

Until 3 October a certificate carried the external potential only as Fourier coefficients written by the untrusted driver, and the checker certified whatever problem those coefficients described (assumption A-DFT-05): a driver that computed the wrong potential produced a self-consistent certificate that the checker accepted. Since 4 October every certificate the driver writes carries a pot line naming the potential — free electrons, a cosine V₀ cos(2πRx/L) or the periodised Gaussian double well, with the exact doubles used for V₀ and σ and the number of copies R per cell — and the checker compares the coefficients with Lean’s own definition of that potential. Lean also proves that these coefficients are the Fourier series of the real-space potential (DFT-V07), so they are not one more convention to trust.

LineWhat it means
PROBLEM …The problem being certified, in exact decimals: the potential and its parameters, L, K, electron pairs, number of k-points and, by certificate kind, temperature, smearing, exchange-correlation and grid size, and for LSDA the electron count, spin and Zeeman field. Echoed, not recomputed; make verify-all compares it with the command line.
POT_OK exactFree electrons and cosine: every coefficient equals Lean’s, so the certified problem is the named problem — at the Γ point (DFT-I02) and, for k-point supercells, the per-cell problem (DFT-I03). Every result of the certificate is then a statement about the named potential.
POT_OK gauss dGaussian double well: its coefficients involve √(2π) and exp, so the checker encloses Lean’s exact coefficients in rational intervals and proves an ℓ¹ deviation of at most d (about 10⁻¹⁵). Variational certificates — zero or finite temperature, Γ point or k-points — also print GAUSS_OK intervals about the named potential, widened by 2N·d (DFT-V09).
POT_OK file d · POT_OK sites dSince 7 October, your own potential: --pot file --potfile F (exact rational Fourier coefficients) or --pot sites --sites F|t:V0:σ,… (periodised Gaussian sites). The certificate carries the data as exact fractions and the checker echoes every value (PROBLEM POTC, PROBLEM SITE); given the same --potfile or --sites it re-reads the input itself and rejects a mismatch. Dyadic coefficients bind exactly (POT_OK exact); non-dyadic ones and sites give an exactly computed deviation d and then the same GAUSS_… lines as the Gaussian well (DFT-V18–V24). Not yet: the cutoff limit for sites, LSDA and zero-temperature LDA.
POT_FAILThe coefficients are not those of the named potential, or the certificate has no pot line at all (since 5 October the binding is mandatory, DFT-I04); nothing else is printed.

Since 5 October the pot line is mandatory (DFT-I04): the checker runs the binding first for every kind of certificate and rejects one without a pot line, so a driver cannot escape the binding by leaving it out. The archived showcase and research certificates, written before the binding, now carry the pot line too (regenerated, or added after checking that their problem data match the current driver line by line). Since 6 October a proved perturbation estimate (DFT-V10) moves the other Gaussian results to the named potential: changing the potential by d in ℓ¹ shifts every Hamiltonian by a Toeplitz matrix of norm at most d, so the checker re-runs the Newton–Kantorovich test with the widened residual and prints GAUSS_SCF/GAUSS_LDA (a unique SCF solution of the named problem, rHF or LDA, Γ point or k-points), GAUSS_EIG, GAUSS_RHO, GAUSS_BAND, GAUSS_DOS and GAUSS_ENTROPY/GAUSS_ENERGY (DFT-V11–V14). The price is a wider interval: for a double well with Marzari–Vanderbilt smearing (d ≈ 1.3×10⁻¹⁵) the radius of uniqueness grows from 1.3×10⁻¹² to 2.6×10⁻¹¹ and the energy interval from 3.7×10⁻¹⁰ to 7.6×10⁻⁹. Caveats, as in the ledger: zero-temperature LDA (fis-lda0) and the zero-temperature energy components HAR_OK, EXT_OK, KIN_OK still speak about the certificate’s coefficients (within d), and the k-point truncation limit has no bound for the Gaussian tail. What remains trusted is that Lean’s definition of the named potentials matches the one the specification describes.

Research result: several exact SCF solutions

The rHF energy is convex, so its SCF solution is unique. LDA exchange is concave, and uniqueness can fail — but a floating-point SCF that lands on different points from different starting densities does not prove it: they could be approximations of one solution, or of none. SCF-solution certificates can. Each proves that exactly one solution lies in a ball around its centre, so accepted certificates for the same problem whose balls are pairwise disjoint prove as many distinct exact solutions.

For a 1D LDA electron gas (Slater exchange with PW92 correlation) with four electrons per period L = 0.6, a cosine potential of period L/4 (V₀ = 20), K = 6, M = 25 grid points and Fermi–Dirac smearing at T = 0.03, five certificates with byte-identical problem data are accepted by the Lean checker. Their radii are at most 1.6×10⁻¹² and their centres 0.048–0.096 apart, so the discrete SCF equations have at least five distinct exact solutions, with pairwise different densities. Disjoint free-energy intervals also order them: the symmetric solution is not the lowest.

Mechanism: the half-filled shell k = ±2π/L forms a charge-density wave under exchange, and the potential, which has only frequency-4 components, pins its phase at a few discrete values; φ = 0 and π are related only by a shift the 25-point grid does not have, so they are genuinely different solutions. Limits: the result is about this discrete problem (K = 6, M = 25) — floating-point runs find five solutions at K = 8, 12 and 16 too, without certificates; it is a lower bound on the number of solutions, not a count, and which are stable is not proved; it is an open-shell effect of a small cell at the Γ point, not a bulk instability. The certificates predate the problem binding and have no pot line, which does not affect the conclusion since all five share the same problem data. They are archived in research/kohn-sham-multiplicity/ and re-checked by make verify-all.

Open gaps

An output without a Lean theorem is unfinished. These are the ones we know about, listed rather than hidden.

  • ForcesSince 8 October certify prints certified force intervals for Gaussian sites (--pot sites) on rHF Γ-point certificates at zero temperature and with Fermi–Dirac or Gaussian smearing (FORCE_OK, FORCE_SUM, DFT-N05–N08), resting on the Hellmann–Feynman theorem proved on 7 October (DFT-N01–N03). The half-width is set by the density error and is about the square root of the energy width (1.8×10⁻⁴ at K = 16). Missing: k-point certificates (UNSUPPORTED FORCE), SCF-solution certificates (MV, MP, --scf-cert), LDA and LSDA (the derivative along a locally unique SCF branch needs the implicit function theorem, DFT-N09), and the well centres of --pot gauss (floating-point forces only; the same potential written as two --pot sites sites is certified).
  • k-point sums versus the infinite crystalEach k-point run is certified for its own discrete problem, but the error of the k-point sum relative to the infinite crystal has no bound yet; k-point energies are not variational.
  • Grids containing the Brillouin-zone boundaryNo certificate; DFTK’s treatment there is also a different discrete problem, so neither side is compared.
  • Scaling of SCF-solution certificatesChecking time grows roughly with the fourth power of the supercell size; the derivative bounds used for higher-order Methfessel–Paxton are several times the true maxima and loosen as the order rises; for gapped systems with the chemical potential mid-gap the SCF equations are ill-conditioned and the certificate honestly fails; uniqueness is only local, and for LDA several solutions do exist (above).
  • LDA limitsZero temperature is certified only when the gap stays open (a metal at zero temperature has no certificate); k-point LDA certificates need M ≥ 4K + 3 (the default 4K + 1 only agrees with DFTK); near-insulating systems (∂N/∂μ ≈ 0) fail the SCF-solution certificate; the LDA energy interval is a first-order estimate.
  • Collinear spin (LSDA)Certified with spin-polarised Slater exchange and with spin-interpolated PW92 correlation, each with an optional uniform Zeeman field: finite temperature at Γ and with k-points (SCF solution, magnetisation, μ, energy, free energy, per-spin bands and DOS), zero temperature at Γ and with k-points under a common gap, and the named Gaussian potential at Γ. The PW92 energy interval uses uniform constants on [lo, ∞)², which are about two orders of magnitude above the true maxima, so it is one to two orders wider than for Slater exchange; physical uniqueness (DFT-T34, T37) is proved for Slater exchange only. Missing: user sites and non-dyadic coefficient files (rejected), zero-temperature bands and DOS, the gapless or fully polarised zero-temperature case, Gaussian LSDA with k-points (rejected) and Gaussian LSDA bands (the band lines of a Gaussian certificate are about its rounded coefficients). For the Gaussian potential the Newton–Kantorovich condition can fail for the named potential although it holds for the certificate (double well V₀ = 2 at T = 0.05: GAUSS_FAIL). DFTK’s Magnetic term, a vector potential, is not implemented.
  • Exact exchange (Hartree–Fock)Certified: the HF SCF solution and its chemical potential at finite temperature and the Γ point, for exact potentials (DFT-H08). Missing: the energy and free-energy interval (DFT-H09), zero temperature, k-points (the Bloch Fock operator needs pair densities at q ≠ 0), the named Gaussian potential and hybrids. Checking time grows like n⁵ (the unknowns are the n² coordinates of the mean-field matrix), so the driver allows K ≤ 12; gapped systems at small T fail honestly (cosine potential at T = 0.05), and the benchmarks use T = 0.3–0.4. DFTK’s own HF SCF does not converge on these 1D systems, so the DFTK comparison is a fixed-point check, not an SCF-to-SCF comparison.
