Formal in Silico

Triple correctness for computer simulation: physical, mathematical, software

Materials design, drug screening and structural safety assessment rely more and more on numbers produced by simulation. Those numbers start from equations and pass through discretisation, iterative algorithms and parallel programs before they land as IEEE 754 floating-point values. A simulated result is trustworthy only if it is correct in three ways at once: physically (the model describes the real world), mathematically (the model’s equations are solved, with bounded error) and in software (the program faithfully executes the algorithm, with bounded floating-point error). Today the three are vouched for by experiment, numerical experience and testing, and they are entangled: when a result disagrees with experiment, nobody can say whether the model, the discretisation or the code is at fault. To our knowledge this programme is the first to establish all three for the same computation: mathematical and software correctness by machine-checked proofs (Lean; TLA+, Frama-C and proved checkers), physical correctness by pre-registered, traceable observations, all in one ledger. Once the mathematical and software errors are rigorously bounded, what is left of a disagreement with experiment is model error and measurement uncertainty only, so model error is bounded rigorously instead of being estimated on the assumption that the numerics have converged.

      • DFTCrystal symmetry, proved. The SCF maps of the k-point blocks are equivariant under the space-group operations of the crystal (with fractional translations), so the unique solution in the certificate's ball is symmetric and the equation on the irreducible k points has the same solution as the full one (DFT-K11–K14, T3). A point-group mode of certify-pw3d checks the operations itself, assumes no symmetry of the mesh, and concludes about every k point, bands included (K15–K18, T4). The Si model passes on the 8×8×8 mesh the S1 protocol requires (29 of 512 k points, 80 CPU hours) and on 4×4×4 at 410 plane waves (276 CPU hours); all 8192 DFTK eigenvalues of the 8×8×8 case lie inside. Against time reversal alone the CPU time drops threefold; the extrapolated cost of the S1 certificate is about 1000 CPU hours.
      • DFTBand bounds over the whole Brillouin zone. From a passing SCF certificate, a new mode of certify-pw3d bounds the bands of the effective Hamiltonian at any k point, off the mesh included, and bounds the highest valence band over the whole zone from an octree of cubes with a gradient bound for the nonlocal kernel (DFT-W30–W40; the checker theorems at T4).     §1

    Why do this

    Scientific software usually earns trust in three ways: comparison with analytic solutions, with experiment, and with other programs. All three work, but they only cover the cases that were tested, and when a result deviates it is hard to say which layer the deviation came from.

    1991 · FEM

    The Sleipner A platform sinks

    The concrete base of the Sleipner A platform in the North Sea failed during a ballast test and sank. The investigation found that poorly shaped elements in the linear-elastic finite-element analysis underestimated the shear stress in the tricell walls by about 47%, so the reinforcement was insufficient. The program ran normally and reported no error.

    Lesson: mesh quality and discretisation error should be checkable preconditions, not a matter of engineering judgement.

    2016 · DFT

    The Δ-gauge and reproducibility

    Lejaeghere et al. compared the PBE equations of state of 71 elemental crystals across 15 solid-state codes and 40 pseudopotential or basis-set schemes. Recent methods agree well; older schemes deviate noticeably, and the source of each difference could only be located by item-by-item comparison.

    Lesson: results depend on many implicit numerical settings. Those settings belong in the specification, tied to an error estimate.

    Since 1983 · DFT

    The band-gap problem

    Kohn–Sham band gaps from LDA and GGA systematically underestimate measured gaps: about 0.5 eV for silicon against an experimental 1.17 eV, and germanium comes out almost gapless in LDA. Perdew and Levy, and Sham and Schlüter, showed in 1983 that the cause is the derivative discontinuity of the exchange–correlation potential, not the numerics: carry the computation to any precision and the discrepancy stays.

    Lesson: the numerics can be entirely correct and the conclusion still wrong. Physical correctness can only be tested by observation, and only when the mathematical and software errors are rigorously bounded can a discrepancy with experiment be pinned on the model itself.

    Parallelism makes things harder. An MD code decomposes space over thousands of MPI ranks; if an atom is lost or duplicated as it crosses a subdomain boundary, the energy curve may show only a barely visible jump. The order of floating-point reductions changes with the number of ranks, so the same input gives bitwise-different trajectories on machines of different size. These errors have nothing to do with physics. They are errors in concurrent protocols, and concurrent protocols are exactly what TLA+ is good at.

    The examples are three different kinds of error: Sleipner was a mathematical error (uncontrolled discretisation), a lost atom is a software error, and the band gap is a physical error in the model itself. Common practice checks all three the same way, by comparing with experiment or with other programs, so a deviation cannot be located. This programme separates them: each correctness gets its own kind of evidence and its own entries in the ledger.

    §2

    Core claim: triple correctness, and a result is only as trustworthy as the weakest link

    A simulated conclusion is trustworthy only if it is correct in three ways at once; none can be left out:

    • Physically correct. The model (equations, functional, force field, constitutive law, thermodynamic database) describes the real world in the range it claims. This cannot be proved; only experimental observation can confirm or refute it, always with a measurement uncertainty.
    • Mathematically correct. What is solved really is the model’s equations: the solution exists and is unique, and the discretisation and iteration errors have rigorous bounds. Machine proofs in Lean 4 guarantee this.
    • Software correct. The program faithfully executes the algorithm, parallel protocols lose and duplicate nothing, and floating-point rounding error is bounded. TLA+ specifications and model checking, Frama-C proofs of the C code and proved certificate checkers guarantee this.

    Trust depends on the weakest link. We split the path from the real world to a floating-point number into seven layers and write an explicit obligation between each pair: the first is physical (validation), the middle three are mathematical (PDE theory and numerical analysis), the last two belong to software (specification and floating-point analysis). To our knowledge this is the first time the three correctnesses have been put into one framework and one ledger.

    1. L0

      Real world

      Experimental observations: band gaps, lattice constants, magnetic moments, phase diagrams, elastic moduli, diffusion coefficients, with their measurement conditions and uncertainties.

    2. Physics Validation: the model’s predictions agree with the observations within the stated uncertainty, and agreement is claimed only in the range where it was checked
    3. L1

      Physical model

      The equations themselves: Kohn–Sham, Newton or Langevin equations of motion, linear elasticity.

    4. Maths Well-posedness: a solution exists, is unique and depends continuously on the data
    5. L2

      Continuous problem

      A variational form in a function space, e.g. find u ∈ H¹ with a(u, v) = ℓ(v) for all v.

    6. Maths Discretisation error estimate: uh → u, with a rate
    7. L3

      Discrete problem

      A finite-dimensional algebraic system: stiffness matrices, plane-wave coefficients, particle coordinates.

    8. Maths Algorithmic convergence: when the iteration stops at step k, the algebraic error is controlled
    9. L4

      Abstract algorithm

      SCF iteration, velocity Verlet, conjugate gradients — described mathematically, in exact arithmetic.

    10. TLA+ Refinement: every step of the parallel program corresponds to one step of the abstract algorithm, or leaves the abstract state unchanged
    11. L5

      Parallel program

      MPI ranks, messages, shared-memory threads, GPU streams, checkpoint and restart.

    12. Floating point Rounding-error bound: the distance between the floating-point result and the exact-arithmetic result
    13. L6

      Floating-point execution

      The number actually computed on IEEE 754 hardware.

    ‖u − ũh(k)‖ ≤ ‖u − uh‖ + ‖uh − uh(k)‖ + ‖uh(k) − ũh(k)‖
    • Maths Discretisation and algebraic error: from PDE theory and the theory of the algorithm
    • Floating point Rounding error: from floating-point analysis
    • TLA+ Precondition: the program really computes ũh(k) — guaranteed by refinement

    This inequality is the backbone of mathematical and software correctness. Each term must be backed by a theorem, and the premise that makes it hold — that the parallel program faithfully executes the abstract algorithm — must be guaranteed by the software-layer specification. Physical correctness adds one more layer outside it:

    yobs − ỹ = (yobs − ytrue) + (ytrue − y(u)) + (y(u) − ỹ)
    • Physics Measurement error and model error: only experiment can confirm them, with a statistical uncertainty
    • Maths Numerical and software error: enclosed rigorously by the inequality above, y(u) ∈ [lo, hi]

    When computation is compared with experiment today, the three terms are mixed: a deviation may come from the model, or from the cutoff, the k-points, SCF convergence, the pseudopotential or the code. Once the last term is enclosed by a certificate, subtracting a known interval from the deviation yobs − ỹ leaves only model error and measurement error: model error is bounded rigorously, not estimated. Here the model is everything fixed at L1: the equations, the functional or force field, the pseudopotential, and the identification of the computed quantity with the measured one (for example a Kohn–Sham gap standing in for an optical gap). That is what the three correctnesses gain from being together, and what distinguishes this programme from pure verification or pure validation. The rules and grades for validation are in §5.

    §3

    Three kinds of evidence: mathematics, software and physics layers, one bridge, one foundation

    Mathematics layer

    PDE theory and numerical analysis

    • Well-posedness: Lax–Milgram, inf-sup conditions, existence of minimisers of the Kohn–Sham energy
    • Discrete convergence: a priori estimates (Céa’s lemma, interpolation, plane-wave truncation) and a posteriori estimates
    • Structure preservation: symplecticity, time reversibility, conservation of momentum and charge, crystal symmetry
    • Iterative convergence: conditions and stopping criteria for SCF fixed points, Newton and Krylov methods

    Tool: Lean 4 with Mathlib, which already has Hilbert spaces, measure theory and Lax–Milgram. Strategy: state theorems first as axioms with a literature source so that upper layers can depend on them at once, then replace the axioms with machine proofs. Every unproved axiom is registered in the trust ledger.

    Software layer

    TLA+ specification and model checking

    • Safety: atoms are never lost or duplicated, every degree of freedom has exactly one owner, parallel assembly has no write conflicts, no deadlock
    • Liveness: messages are eventually delivered; the SCF loop either converges or reports failure in finitely many steps
    • Protocols: domain decomposition and halo exchange, distributed FFT transposes, checkpoint and restart, adaptive refinement and load rebalancing

    Tools: PlusCal for algorithms; TLC for exhaustive checking of small instances; Apalache for symbolic checking; TLAPS for proofs at any scale. Link to the code: the implementation emits an event log at run time, and trace validation checks that the log is a behaviour the specification allows.

    Bridge

    Refinement carries the theorems to the parallel program

    The abstract algorithm is a TLA+ specification A whose state is the global density or phase-space point, and whose steps are one mathematical iteration each. The parallel implementation is a specification P whose state is spread over processes. A refinement mapping projects the state of P onto that of A — for example by stitching local densities into the global one. Once P ⇒ A is proved, the convergence theorems about A hold for the parallel program.

    Performance work is handled the same way: every optimisation is a new refinement, and must be proved to still implement the level above.

    Physics layer

    Validation: traceable observations and certified model error

    • What is validated: the model’s predictions of observables (band gaps, lattice constants, bulk moduli, magnetic moments, phase boundaries, elastic response, transport coefficients) agree with experiment in the stated range and uncertainty
    • Where they disagree: a certified model error, the observed value minus the rigorous computed interval, with the measurement uncertainty on top
    • Range of validity: which compositions, temperatures, pressures and structures were checked, and where there are no data

    Data: every experimental value is registered with its source (DOI), conditions, uncertainty and the corrections needed before comparison (zero-point and temperature corrections for band gaps, zero-point expansion for lattice constants); data files are hashed and versioned with the repository. Rules: the quantities, tolerances and data sets are fixed before the comparison; data used to fit a model are not reused to validate it; a conclusion holds only in the validated range. Limits: physical correctness can be corroborated or refuted, never proved; measurement uncertainty is statistical, unlike the rigorous intervals of the other layers, and the two are reported separately.

    Foundation

    Floating point is a first-class citizen

    IEEE 754 addition is not associative. Floating-point results never enter a conclusion directly: the output of a large solver goes to a proved checker that uses either exact rational arithmetic or outward-rounded interval arithmetic. The only floating-point fact we then have to trust is “round-to-nearest returns the nearest double”.

    Global reductions use a deterministic summation order, so results do not depend on the number of processes or threads. The rounding model is part of the specification and sits in the same error budget as the discretisation and algebraic errors.

    §5

    Trust tiers and validation grades

    Not everything can be proved at once. Every component states honestly which tier it has reached and lists the assumptions it depends on in the trust ledger. Tiers only go up, and are never overstated.

    T0

    Tested

    Regression tests, analytic solutions, comparison with established codes. Holds only for the inputs tested.

    T1

    Paper proof

    A proof on paper with a literature source and explicit assumptions, not yet machine-checked.

    T2

    Model-checked

    TLC or Apalache has exhausted every behaviour of a finite instance.

    T3

    Machine proof

    A Lean, Rocq or TLAPS proof valid at any scale, for the abstract model, exact arithmetic or a line-by-line model of the code.

    T4

    End to end

    Machine proof all the way down to executable code and floating-point rounding. Precedent: Boldo et al.’s proof of a C program for the 1D wave equation.

    T0–T4 measure mathematical and software correctness. Physical correctness cannot be proved, so it is measured on a separate axis, the validation grades V0–V3. A conclusion carries both, for example “band-gap interval T4, comparison with experiment V2”. Every result so far is at V0: the ledger has no validation claims yet, and the physical track is planned (§8).

    V0

    Not validated

    No comparison with observation. All one can say is “the model gives this number”.

    V1

    Single comparison

    Compared with experimental data that have a source and an uncertainty; the deviation is reported together with the computed interval and the measurement uncertainty.

    V2

    Systematic validation

    Compared on a data set and protocol fixed in advance, separate from the fitting data, giving the distribution of model errors and the validated range.

    V3

    Blind prediction

    The computed interval is archived, time-stamped and with its certificate, before the measurement, and then confirmed or refuted by an independent experiment.

    §6

    Principles

    Specification before code

    Every module has a mathematical statement and a TLA+ specification before it has an implementation.

    Assumptions are visible

    Regularity, mesh conditions, step-size limits and convergence thresholds are written into the specification, not hidden in input-file defaults.

    Honest tiers

    Better to mark T0 than to overstate T3. The trust ledger is the most important file in the project.

    A small trusted kernel

    Only a few kernels need T4; upper layers inherit their guarantees through composition theorems.

    Floating point enters the specification

    The rounding model and its error bounds join the discretisation error in a single error budget.

    Reproducible by default

    The same input gives bitwise-identical results at any process count, unless the user explicitly gives this up.

    Correct first, fast later

    First obtain a provable version, then optimise without breaking the proof. Every optimisation is a new refinement.

    Verify the checker, not the solver

    Large numerical libraries such as LAPACK or FFT libraries are not proved; their outputs are checked by proved checkers. Solvers are replaceable; the guarantee comes from the checker.

    Every run comes with a report

    Each line of the report cites a claim in the trust ledger, from which its tier and assumptions can be looked up; the report ends with the assumptions the run used.

    Every output has a theorem

    Every number the program prints must correspond to a machine-proved theorem. Where the result is an approximation — iteration, discretisation, k-point sampling, finite temperature, floating point — what is proved is an error bound. Tests, model checking and sanitizers are supporting evidence only. An output without such a theorem is unfinished and is listed as a gap.

    The three correctnesses are kept apart

    Mathematical and software conclusions carry T tiers, physical conclusions V grades, and reports state them separately. Rigorous intervals and statistical uncertainties are never mixed.

    Experimental data are traceable

    Every experimental value used for validation has a source, conditions, an uncertainty and documented corrections; fitting and validation sets are kept apart, and the comparison protocol is fixed before the comparison.

    Open

    Specifications, proofs, benchmarks, experimental data sets and the trust ledger are to be public, so that anyone can re-check them.

    §7

    What we do not do

    Claim to prove a physical model

    Physical correctness can only be confirmed by observation; its conclusions always carry a measurement uncertainty and hold only in the validated range (the V grades of §5). We measure model error, but developing new functionals, force fields or constitutive laws is not a goal. Versions before 8 October 2026 excluded validation altogether (“we do not judge whether the physical model is correct”); that non-goal is withdrawn, and validation is now one of the three correctnesses.

    Replace existing software at the outset

    Quantum ESPRESSO, VASP, LAMMPS, GROMACS, deal.II and FEniCS are benchmarks and references, not targets for replacement.

    Prove all the code

    Input parsing, I/O and visualisation can stay at T0, as long as they do not enter the error budget.

    §8

    Roadmap

    Current goal — a 3D showcase of triple correctness set 8 Oct 2026

    Extend DFT to three-dimensional real materials and give, on the same systems, evidence for all three correctnesses: mathematical (existence and uniqueness of the SCF solution, rigorous intervals for energies and band gaps, where possible for the continuous problem), software (TLA+ specifications and trace validation of the parallel protocols, proved checkers) and physical (systematic validation against experimental band gaps, lattice constants and magnetic moments, with a certified exchange–correlation model error). Planned showcases, none with results yet:

    • Physics S1 Certified model error. LDA (later PBE) band gaps and lattice constants of semiconductors and insulators against experiment; the computed values are rigorous intervals, so the deviation is the error of the model (functional and pseudopotential, with the Kohn–Sham gap standing in for the measured gap) — a band-gap problem free of numerical error.
    • Maths S2 Not the ground state. Prove by machine-checked certificate that a converged self-consistent solution is not the ground state of the discrete model: the higher solution is certified, and a trial state has a strictly lower free-energy bound.     §9

    First milestones: mini-DFT and mini-FEM

    Before extending to MD and FEM, we walk through L1 to L6 with the smallest DFT program we could design. It is small enough that every layer’s obligation can be written down, yet it keeps the full skeleton of a Kohn–Sham calculation: a nonlinear eigenvalue problem, self-consistent iteration, plane-wave discretisation.

    ModelOne-dimensional periodic reduced Hartree–Fock (rHF), no exchange-correlation. Periodic interval [0, L), N electrons, spin-degenerate. Energy: kinetic + external + ½ Hartree, with the Hartree kernel 4π/q² on nonzero frequencies.
    PotentialSmooth periodic: constant, cosine, periodised Gaussian double well (optionally repeated). The 1D Coulomb potential is not integrable at the origin and is not used.
    DiscretisationPlane waves with |k| ≤ K; Γ only, or Nk equally weighted k-points (Γ-centred or Monkhorst–Pack). A real-space grid of at least 4K + 1 points guarantees that density and Hamiltonian action are alias-free.
    OccupationZero temperature with a gap checked in interval arithmetic at every iteration; or Fermi–Dirac, Gaussian, Methfessel–Paxton or Marzari–Vanderbilt smearing with a common chemical potential.
    AlgorithmSCF with linear or Anderson mixing and optional Kerker preconditioning; dense diagonalisation; shared-memory thread parallelism over k-points with a fixed summation order.

    Why rHF

    Its energy is convex in the density matrix, and the Hartree term is strictly convex on nonzero frequencies, so the ground-state density is unique. Within the DFT family it has the cleanest mathematics — a good first target for machine proof. Adding LDA exchange-correlation loses convexity; that step belongs to phase 1.

    LAPACK solves, the checker guarantees

    LAPACK stays at T0. After convergence the solver writes a certificate; a checker written and compiled in Lean re-checks it in exact rational arithmetic and returns rigorous intervals for the energy, free energy, truncation limit, density, eigenvalues, band structure and density of states. Its soundness is a Lean theorem: it trusts no number from the solver, so a solver bug can only make the check fail or the interval wider.

    mini-FEM follows the same pattern for goal G2: 1D Poisson with P1 elements (Dirichlet, Neumann and Robin boundaries) and the beam equation with cubic Hermite elements (clamped, pinned, free and sliding ends), with right-hand sides built from polynomials, exponentials, sines and cosines. The Lean checker returns intervals for the true solution of the continuous problem at every node, and a rigorous bound on the maximum error over the whole interval. Adaptive refinement uses these proved element-wise bounds as its marking criterion.

    mini-MD (1 October 2026) does the same for a 1D Lennard-Jones chain with velocity Verlet: the checker bounds the distance from the true solution of Newton’s equations at every step, and from the initial state over about one vibration period. mini-CALPHAD (goal G3) certifies binary phase equilibria from TDB databases — magnetic phases and whole phase diagrams included — with the chemical potentials, driving forces and which phases can appear in any equilibrium. See the MD, CALPHAD and CSP pages.

    mini-CSP (7 October 2026) starts the structure-search track with Lennard-Jones clusters: an untrusted searcher proposes candidates, and the checker certifies each as an exact local minimum, unique up to rotation, reflection and translation, and certifies pairs of candidates as equal or distinct. The run’s BEST is the lowest certified minimum it found; global optimality is not claimed.

    Decisions and open questions

    D1 · decided 27 Sep 2026 · revised 29 Sep 2026 · code-level tool decided 6 Oct 2026

    Provers: Lean 4 for the mathematics and the floating-point model; Frama-C’s floating-point model at the code level

    Division
    • Lean 4 + Mathlib for L1–L4 (well-posedness, discretisation error, structure preservation, iterative convergence) and for two parts of L6: the certificate checkers (exact rationals, compiled to executables) and a line-by-line binary64 model of the interval-arithmetic kernel.
    • Frama-C/WP for the absence of run-time errors in the trusted C kernel.
    • The code-level link (decided 6 Oct 2026): Frama-C/WP’s floating-point model. The interval kernel’s ACSL contracts state, over real numbers, what the Lean theorems state; WP translates every double operation into Why3’s IEEE 754 binary64 theory and Alt-Ergo, CVC5 and Z3 prove every goal on the actual C code (DFT-F01, F02 at T4). For the inertia count, Frama-C proves that the intervals enclose an exact symmetric-pivoting elimination; Lean proves that this elimination counts the eigenvalues.
    Why
    The mathematics layer is the bulk of the work; Mathlib’s analysis and measure theory form one unified library, and most AI-assisted proving tools support Lean first. “Verify the checker, not the solver” shrinks the floating-point obligations enough that a small binary64 model in Lean suffices. For the code level, Frama-C was chosen because it already proves the kernel’s run-time safety, so one annotation file and one proof run extend to floating point, with no third prover and no second C semantics; WP’s IEEE 754 model and Alt-Ergo’s floating-point theory discharge the rounding goals automatically; and without Rocq the Lean/Rocq interface rule and claim DFT-I01 are withdrawn.
    Cost
    That the ACSL statements say what the Lean statements say is checked by hand (an assumption in the ledger). The trusted base grows by WP’s floating-point model, a contract for nextafter, Frama-C’s libc contracts for sqrt, fabs and isfinite, three SMT solvers, and one admit for the value of INFINITY, which WP cannot translate. The SMT proofs depend on hand-written invariants and hints, and take about five minutes on a 384-core machine.

    D2 · decided 27 Sep 2026

    Implementation language: C11, split into a proved kernel and an untrusted driver

    Division
    • Trusted kernel — roots of unity, grid density and charge intervals, interval-arithmetic residual bounds and inertia counting. Restricted C with formal annotations; Frama-C/WP proves no run-time errors and the floating-point correctness of the interval arithmetic (T4).
    • Certificate checkers in Lean, exact rationals, soundness a Lean theorem.
    • Driver — SCF loop, LAPACK, threads, I/O, reports, event logs, certificate output. Stays at T0: every number it computes must pass the kernel or a checker before it appears as a guaranteed result.
    • Python and Julia appear only in benchmarks and trace-validation tooling, outside the trust chain.
    Why
    The mature code-level verification routes all start from C. LAPACK and FFTW have C interfaces. The main risk, memory safety, is handled by proof in the kernel and by sanitizers in the driver. Compilation forbids fused multiply-add contraction and any fast-math optimisation, so the compiled arithmetic matches the proofs.
    Not chosen
    Rust (its verifiers do not connect to the code-level tools above), Julia or Python (no route to code-level proof), Fortran (few verification tools), Lean itself (its floating point has no formal semantics).

    Open questions

    • Continuum theory of 1D rHF. Resolved at T1 on 5 October 2026: no published result covers the 1D periodic Hartree kernel directly, so the project supplies paper proofs (existence and unique density at zero and finite temperature, the cutoff limit equals the continuum energy, exponential plane-wave convergence for analytic potentials). What remains is to machine-check them, which ties in with the next question. The cutoff limit was machine-checked on 8 October 2026 (DFT-C02, T3) with the continuum model written in Fourier space, which avoids periodic Sobolev spaces; its equivalence with the operator model on H¹ is assumption A-DFT-08.
    • Comparing with experiment. Measured and model quantities rarely mean the same thing: band gaps need zero-point and temperature corrections, lattice constants zero-point expansion, and the corrections come from other models or measurements. How they enter a V grade without contaminating the rigorous part, and how to keep fitting and validation sets apart when published functionals and databases do not always say what they were fitted to, are open.
    • How deep to formalise Sobolev spaces — what to axiomatise first, and which theorems to prove first.
    • Randomness. Langevin dynamics and random initial guesses involve probabilistic properties; TLA+ only describes nondeterminism.
    • GPUs. How asynchronous streams and weak memory models enter a TLA+ specification.
