mini-CSP

Crystal structure prediction, starting with Lennard-Jones clusters. The searcher — random generation, relaxation, particle-swarm updates, deduplication, the same ingredients as CALYPSO, written from the published papers — is untrusted. Every candidate it proposes goes to the Lean checker certify-csp, which proves it an exact local minimum, unique up to rotation, reflection and translation, with a rigorous energy interval, and proves pairs of candidates equal or distinct. 01

The problem

SystemN identical atoms in three dimensions (2 ≤ N ≤ 200), a free cluster at zero pressure. Every pair interacts through the Lennard-Jones potential, written in the squared distance s = r²: φ(s) = 4ε(σ¹²/s⁶ − σ⁶/s³), not truncated.
EnergyE(x) = ½ Σi≠j φ(sij). Energy, gradient and Hessian are rational functions of the coordinates, so the checker evaluates exactly the formula Lean reasons about (CSP-C01).
SymmetryE is invariant under rotations and reflections, translations and relabelling of the atoms. A minimum is therefore never isolated in ℝ3N; it is a minimum modulo these motions, and the six orbit directions are fixed by the Eckart gauge.
Inputε, σ and every coordinate are exact dyadic rationals m·2e in the certificate — the doubles the searcher actually produced. The checker echoes the problem as a PROBLEM line for the caller to compare.

03

How the certificate works

The searcher stops at a point x₀ with a tiny but non-zero gradient. The certificate proves that a true minimum x* lies within a radius it states, by showing that the energy is strongly convex on a small ball in the right slice of configuration space. All checks are exact rational arithmetic; the searcher’s floating-point numbers are only data.

  1. 1

    Eckart gauge

    Rotations and translations make the Hessian singular in six directions. The checker works in the Eckart section through x₀: displacements with no net translation and no net rotation relative to x₀. Lean proves every configuration can be rotated and translated into this section without moving further from x₀, so a strict minimum in the section is a strict minimum modulo Euclidean motions (CSP-D01).

  2. 2

    Atoms stay apart

    On the ball of radius R no pair moves by more than √2·R, so every squared distance stays above an explicit slo > 0. From it the checker computes, for every pair, how much the Hessian can drop across the ball, using explicit bounds on φ″ and φ‴ and the mean-value theorem.

  3. 3

    Small gradient

    ‖∇E(x₀)‖ ≤ g, with every term rounded down to a 2⁻¹⁰⁰ grid and the rounding error added to the bound.

  4. 4

    Positive definite, exactly

    The Hessian at x₀ minus the pair losses, plus a penalty κ on the six orbit directions, minus μ·I, is shown positive semi-definite by a Schur-complement elimination that rounds to a 2⁻¹⁰⁰ grid at every step and subtracts the rounding error from the diagonal (proved sound in Lean). Then E is μ-strongly convex on the whole ball within the section.

  5. 5

    Strong-convexity ball theorem

    If g < μR, the ball contains exactly one stationary point, the unique minimiser, within g/μ of x₀, with energy in [E(x₀) − g²/(2μ), E(x₀)] (CSP-D02). The checker encloses E(x₀) itself, so this is the printed interval.

  6. 6

    Same or distinct

    For a same record the certificate gives a permutation, an exactly orthogonal rational matrix (a signed Cayley transform, checked as QᵀQ = I exactly) and a translation that map one candidate into the other’s uniqueness ball, and vice versa. Two minima whose energy intervals are disjoint are distinct structures without any further data.

Why the intervals are so tight: the searcher finishes each relaxation with Newton steps until the gradient is about 10⁻¹³, so g²/(2μ) is about 10⁻²⁴; the width of the interval is set by the rounding of the 2⁻¹⁰⁰ grid and the outward rounding of the printed decimals. The uniqueness radius is 2×10⁻⁵ to 1.7×10⁻⁴ σ, limited by how fast the Lennard-Jones Hessian changes.

04

The search, and what is proved about it

The searcher build/mini-csp (C11, double precision) follows the algorithm published by Wang, Lv, Zhu and Ma (PRB 82, 094116, 2010; CPC 183, 2063, 2012), written from the papers alone, without reference to any existing program. Each generation relaxes every particle (BFGS, then Newton with a gauge penalty), sorts by energy, replaces the worst fraction with fresh random structures and moves the rest by the particle-swarm update v ← ωv + c₁r₁(p − x) + c₂r₂(g − x), x ← x + v. Sorted interatomic distances serve as a fingerprint, but only to propose merges; only the checker decides.

Nothing the searcher does enters the trust chain: a bug in it can only make it miss structures, never produce a wrong certified statement. Separately, Lean proves two properties of the search algorithm itself, under idealised assumptions stated in the ledger:

  • Particle-swarm stability regions (CSP-A01)With the attractors fixed and ideal uniform random numbers, the mean of a particle converges exactly when |ω| < 1 and 0 < c₁ + c₂ < 4(1 + ω), and its mean square exactly in a smaller region that for c₁ = c₂ is Poli’s 0 < c < 24(1 − ω²)/(7 − 5ω). Both are “if and only if”, proved through the Schur–Cohn/Jury criteria for quadratic and cubic polynomials. The Clerc–Kennedy defaults ω = 0.7298, c₁ = c₂ = 1.49618 — and their exact double values — lie in both regions.
  • Random restarts find the global basin (CSP-A02)If every generation contains at least one independent structure drawn uniformly from a box containing the global minimum, the probability that none has landed within δ of it after t generations is at most (1 − pδ)t → 0, with pδ > 0. Asymptotic only: it gives no guarantee for a finite run.

Acknowledgement. The search algorithm is that of CALYPSO, by Yanchao Wang, Jian Lv, Li Zhu and Yanming Ma (Jilin University). Their clear published description of particle-swarm structure prediction is what made an independent, verifiable implementation possible; mini-CSP is our tribute to that work, not a replacement for it.

07

Open gaps

  • Global optimalityBEST is the lowest certified minimum the run found. Proving it global needs a branch-and-bound certificate over configuration space (planned phase 5, CSP-B05).
  • Crystals, symmetry and pressureOnly free clusters at zero pressure so far. Symmetry-constrained generation with symmetry certificates, periodic cells, pressure and multi-component systems are phases 2 and 3 (CSP-B03, C02, D03, D04).
  • Deduplication in the searchsame records are only produced by --same-demo; merges the fingerprint proposes during a search do not yet come with permutation and rotation certificates, so only one representative is kept (permutation matching plus Kabsch alignment is planned).
  • The searcher’s codeUntrusted (T0). The particle-swarm step is to get a Frama-C/WP proof under the IEEE 754 model (CSP-P01); the search loop, its distribution and restart logic a TLA+ specification with trace validation (CSP-L01); and a fis csp command with JSON output (CSP-L02).
  • Beyond the idealised modelCSP-A01 and A02 assume fixed attractors, ideal random numbers and real coordinates; the mean-square result covers p = g only. The phase-1 searcher uses ω = 0.7, c₁ = c₂ = 1.5, inside both regions by the proved formula but not the constants of defaults_stable, and clamps velocities, which the model does not include.
